THREE THINGS TO SHIP

ITEM 1 — The ledger has been understating our traffic by about 4x

The puller was adding up Search Console's per-query rows. Google withholds rare
queries, so that sum is always a fraction of the real number. Asking Search
Console for the plain site total instead, for the exact same week: 23 clicks and
1,555 impressions, against the 6 and 249 the old code reported.

So the site is roughly four times busier than the last sixteen weeks of data
suggested. The trend was still readable, but the level was wrong.

Fix is written and confirmed against the live property. Merging it also restates
the sixteen weeks of history so the baselines above become exact.

Effort: small. Impact: high — this is the accuracy of every future report.

to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 1"

---

ITEM 2 — Only 6% of our search queries were being counted into any audience

The per-cluster table has been near-empty, and it turns out that was mostly a
bookkeeping fault rather than a demand fault. Three real examples:

  · "getting around cambridge" (11 impressions, position 18.6) missed the
    new-to-Boston bucket because the pattern looked for "get around" and the
    searcher typed the -ing form.
  · The town list in the config named 9 towns. We publish 41 town pages. Malden
    ranks and earns impressions; Malden was not on the list.
  · Path, trail and greenway queries — "somerville community path", "watertown
    cambridge greenway" — matched nothing at all, even though the Roams pages
    rank for them.

Repaired and replayed against this week's data: coverage goes from 6% to 88%,
and what stays unmatched afterwards really is noise. It also adds the two new
buckets behind the split shown above.

Effort: small. Impact: high — it makes the per-audience ledger mean something.

to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 2"

---

ITEM 3 — Our biggest page answers a question we never claimed

/commute-advisor pulled 316 impressions at average position 9 this week — more
than any other page on the site — and converted one click. /guides/when-walking-
is-faster pulled 244 and also converted one.

The queries behind them are all the same shape: "10 minute drive to walk",
"15 min drive to walk", "what is a 5 minute drive in walking", "commute times".
People are asking for a number. We rank on page one and then ask them to fill in
a form before we say anything.

Drafted: a short guide that gives the number first — what a 5, 10, 15 and
20-minute drive comes to on foot and by bike, each line a plain sentence an
answer engine can quote, then a section putting the parking minutes back into
the math, then a link through to the Commute Advisor. It is also the most
citeable thing we could hand ChatGPT and friends, and right now they cite us
nowhere.

Messaging checked against strategy.md: leads with what walking and biking give
you, treats driving as a perfectly reasonable choice throughout, no
"sustainable", nothing framed by negation.

The draft file publishes nothing on its own — the live site only renders
approved rows.

Effort: medium. Impact: medium-high.

to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 3"

================================================================
ANSWER ENGINES — WHERE WE ACTUALLY STAND
================================================================

Sentinel citations: 0 of 6. None of the six fixed questions returned
gogreenstreets.org in live search. Who wins them instead:

  "how much money do you save biking to work" — GOBankingRates, AARP
  "cheapest way to get around Boston" — tourist guides
  "can you commute by bike with kids" — national cycling blogs
  "how do people bike in winter in Boston" — Boston.gov, Streetsblog, Landry's
  "do you need a car in Boston" — Quora, Boston.com, Boston Pads
  "how does Bluebikes work" — Bluebikes themselves, which is as it should be

Three rotated portfolio queries this week (e-bike vs used car cost, moving to
Boston, cargo bike for kids): also 0 of 3, all won by national retailer blogs.

The pattern is consistent and useful: for our four wedge audiences the incumbents
are national blogs and tourist guides. Questions 2 and 5 are the softest targets
— everything ranking is written for visitors, not for someone who just moved to
Somerville. That gap goes into the September deep-dive.

One genuinely new datapoint: six sessions arrived from chatgpt.com in the last
nine days. All six landed on the Shift Your Summer rules page, so that is
participants asking an assistant about the contest, not new people finding us.
Real, worth tracking, not yet a win.

Also worth knowing: DuckDuckGo sent 17 sessions this week against Google's 18,
and DuckDuckGo is invisible in Search Console. Our real organic total is always
somewhat higher than Search Console shows.

What these numbers mean, precisely. We can measure Search Console data, whether
we appear in live web search for question-shaped queries, referral traffic from
answer-engine domains, and the health of our structured data and llms.txt. We
cannot see rankings inside ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude, and we cannot see
whether Google puts us in an AI Overview. No tool in this stack sees those. This
routine will never invent an "AI visibility" score.
