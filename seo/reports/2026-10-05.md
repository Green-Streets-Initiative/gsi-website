## Insights (X is happening → which means Y)

1. **Event pages are now the site's click engine, and named public events are
   the shape that wins.** /events took 27 of 56 clicks; the Allston Open
   Streets listing ranked 5.5 for a city-run event and converted at 2.0%, the
   opposite of last week's Fluff Fest (500 / 0). Difference: Open Streets
   searchers want the *when and where*, which our page answers; festival
   searchers want the organizer. → Growth in events comes from listing the big
   public car-free and community days early with practical detail. Roslindale
   Open Streets (10-18) is already at 77 impressions, pos 5.7.

   `/events/<title>-<date>` slugs, legacy ids 308 → new; verified live). →
   Search Console history for every event splits across two URLs for a few

   **mapped** cohort (old id ↔ new slug), and readable slugs are a confound on
   any event CTR change from here on. No decision needed; it is a measurement
   note.

   at a third of last week's rate** (paid_other 383 sessions / 26 intent =

## Competitor teardown (3 priority clusters)

Fetch notes: cambridgema.gov (400) and traillink.com (403) refused fetches; 5
fetches used of 8.

**events-programs — "open streets boston 2026 schedule".** boston.gov's
announcement (updated 2026-05-21, ~400 words) owns it: four dates, hours,
closure window, activities. No per-event street list, no transit access, no
Event schema. Streetsblog ranks second with news coverage. *What beats it:* a
per-event page with the exact streets, how to get there by T/bike, where to
lock up, and Event schema — which is exactly what our listing pages are, and
why Allston ranked 5.5. Lever: list each city-run car-free day as soon as the
city announces it, with the getting-there detail.

**roams-trails — "how long is the fresh pond loop".** The SERP answer comes
from Harvard College's running page, cambridgema.gov's Fresh Pond FAQ and
komoot/traillink: all give 2.25 mi (2.5 with Kingsley Park). Every result
states the number, so distance-in-title is now table stakes rather than an
edge. *What beats it:* the things those pages don't combine — where to start
by T or bike, surface, what you pass, how long at walking vs riding pace — in
one extractable paragraph near the top.

**year-round-weather — "biking in Boston winter".** Streetsblog's 2022 piece
(~1,500 words, named riders from MassBike and Bikes Not Bombs, dated workshop
listings) and boston.gov's Winter Biking page (updated 2026-01-12, four named
routes, gear advice; says nothing on how bike lanes are cleared). *What beats
it:* one current local page combining the city facts (lanes cleared within 24
hours of snowfall's end; posts pulled on Mass Ave; 311), Bluebikes winter
operations, starter routes, and links to gear — drafted below as SEO item 1.
