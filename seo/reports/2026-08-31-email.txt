=====================================================================
WHAT I FOUND
=====================================================================

1. Google is indexing the tagged copy of our best town pages, not the real one.

   Our own links caused this. The footer's Shift Towns column and the nav
   dropdown appear on every page, and every one of those links carried a
   "?utm_content=" tracking tag. That made the tagged URL the most-linked
   version of the page by a wide margin, and Google followed the link graph
   instead of the canonical tag.

   Over twelve weeks: the tagged Cambridge town page took 337 impressions and
   the clean /shift/towns/cambridge-ma does not appear in Search Console at
   all. Same for Everett, Somerville, Newton, Watertown and Boston — six of our
   nine biggest town pages, 809 impressions sitting on parameterized copies.
   Arlington shows both versions competing, splitting one page's signals in
   two.

   The canonical tags are correct; I verified them live. They are simply losing
   to our own internal linking. Town pages are the strongest organic asset we
   have, which is exactly why this is worth fixing — nothing looks broken.
   That is item 4 below.

2. Our two biggest pages took 472 impressions and converted none of them.

   /guides/when-walking-is-faster (261 impressions, position 8.4) and
   /commute-advisor (211 impressions, position 11.7) both took zero clicks.
   They are the top two pages on the site, both on page one, and between them
   they are the entire click-through story of the week.

   New queries this week: "commute miles", "commute distance", "commute
   radius" — joining last week's "10 minute drive to walk" and "what is a 5
   minute drive in walking". People are asking for a distance-and-time
   conversion and finding a tool that says nothing until they type into it.
   Second consecutive week, same gap, same result. Item 3 from last week is
   the direct answer and is still sitting unshipped.

3. Someone pricing a used cargo e-bike found us — the first sign of the
   used-car wedge.

   Two variants of a search for a used Tern GSD at $3,000 took 10 impressions
   at position 10, and our e-cargo-bike guide jumped from 10 to 38
   impressions. That is precisely the audience we built the used-car-to-e-bike
   cluster for, arriving through a door we did not build. One week is too thin
   to act on, but it is the first organic life from a wedge audience that has
   shown zero impressions since this routine began. Watching it, and feeding
   it into next week's portfolio review.
