THE FINDING: we had been reading 40 rows of a 128-row table
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The weekly data pull asked Search Console for 500 pages and wrote down the
top 40. Those 40 covered 85% of our impressions and stopped at 22 impressions
per page. Everything below that line has been invisible to every report I
have written for you.

I raised the limit. Two things that were already true turned out to have
been wrong in the reports:

1. THE EVENT PAGES ARE THE BEST-CONVERTING THING ON THE SITE.

   Thirty-three event pages, 299 impressions, 7 clicks — a 2.34% click-through
   rate against a 1.03% site average — ranking at positions 3.6 to 7.7.
   Previous reports saw two to four of them and filed the whole class as a
   rounding error. The BU Cycle Kitchen event ranks 4.6. The East Boston
   library free repair workshop ranks 5.2.

   The sitemap lists two event pages. llms.txt lists none. Google found all
   thirty-three anyway, through internal links and the share cards you added
   on the 16th.

2. "ONLY 12 OF 25 ROAMS ARE INDEXED" WAS NEVER TRUE.

   That has been on the watch list since the 14th and it shaped how the Roam
   title experiment was framed. Twenty-four Roam pages earn impressions. The
   other twelve were simply below the old cutoff.

This is the third time a page class has become a major surface while
invisible to the ledger — campus, then Roams, now events. The first two were
missing search-term patterns. This one was an instrument limit, which is
worse, because no amount of portfolio work would have found it.

WHAT ELSE MOVED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

THE ROAM RAMP IS MORE PAGES, NOT YET BETTER PAGES. The class grew from 852 to
1,094 impressions, but the ten pages that were already indexed took exactly
the same 793 impressions as last week and one click fewer. All the growth is
new pages appearing. Your distance-in-the-title change shipped on the 14th,
four days into a seven-day window, so this is "no signal yet" rather than a
bad result. Verdict still 26 October.

CAMPUS IMPRESSIONS HALVED — 1,129 down to 541 — while the rest of the site
held. That is one week and term has just started, so it is a data point, not
a verdict. But it is the first evidence FOR the "September spike" idea rather
than against it. When the campus title experiment is called on 20 October the
honest question is now two questions: did the titles lift click-through, AND
was there a durable audience under the September volume.

POSTHOG ORGANIC SESSIONS MORE THAN DOUBLED, to 71 against a 32 average. I am
flagging rather than celebrating. Search Console agrees on direction but not
size, and the ratio between the two sources jumped. In its favour: those 71
sessions are spread across 25 landing pages with no single page dominating,
which is not the shape a bot makes. Treat next week as the confirmation.

FIRST ANSWER-ENGINE REFERRALS THAT ARE NOT CONTEST TRAFFIC. Three sessions:
one from ChatGPT, one from Perplexity, one from Gemini. Three is noise and I
will not dress it up. What is notable is the shape — one each from three
different engines, where every previous referral came from one engine
pointing at one contest-rules page. It is also the first week after the Roams
and campus sections went into llms.txt. Both true; neither proves the other.

THE DRIVE-TIME GUIDE HAS GENUINELY ZERO IMPRESSIONS at three weeks live. It
is absent from a 128-row table whose floor is one impression, so this is a
real reading now rather than "not in the top 40". That points away from
waiting and toward the structural options when it is called on 12 October.

THE THREE WEDGE-AUDIENCE SEARCHES ARE OWNED BY VENDORS. This week's rotation
checked the head queries for used-car shoppers, parents, and new movers.
Every single result on the first two is a bike manufacturer or retailer blog.
We do not appear on any of the three. That is worth knowing before October:
our opening in those audiences is the local, specific question a vendor blog
cannot answer — the same shape that is already winning on Roams and events —
not the generic head query.

PROPOSED — three items
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

ITEM 1 — Change one word that is now machine-readable

On the 14th I asked for one word to change before the Commute Advisor schema
shipped: an answer there says "the most sustainable way to build consistent
movement". The schema shipped on the 17th and the word went with it, so it is
now inside the markup that answer engines read back as our description of
ourselves. It was used in the "lasting" sense, so it is fine as body copy —
but "durable" says the same thing and costs us nothing.

One word. Effort: trivial. Impact: guardrail, not traffic.
Branch: seo/2026-09-faq-wording
to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 1"

ITEM 2 — Put the event pages in the sitemap and llms.txt

Thirty-three event pages convert at more than double the site average and the
sitemap lists two of them. This adds every approved upcoming event to the
sitemap, and a "What's on" section to llms.txt with dates and locations.
Because it reads the upcoming-events list, a page drops off both the day after
the event happens — no cleanup logic, nothing to maintain.

This is the same move that worked twice this month: the campus pages and the
Roams each got their llms.txt section and the file went from 79 links to 122.

Typecheck is clean. I could not see the rendered output — an unattended run
cannot start a dev server — so it is worth a look at the Vercel preview,
particularly that event titles read well as link text.

Effort: small. Impact: medium-high.
Branch: seo/2026-09-events-discoverable
to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 2"

ITEM 3 — Fix the search-term buckets

Coverage of what people actually searched fell from 88% to 73% in a week, for
two reasons. The Roams bucket was built from the twelve Roam names that were
indexed when I wrote it, so every Roam that appeared afterwards fell straight
back into "unsorted" — the Battle Road Trail alone is 25 impressions on a page
earning 3 clicks. And the event pages had no bucket at all. This regenerates
the Roam names from all 25 published Roams and adds an events bucket.

Tested against this week's real searches: coverage 73% → 81%, unsorted down
from 165 to 116, and the trigger number is unchanged — it sorts existing
traffic rather than inventing any.

Effort: small. Impact: measurement, not traffic directly.
Branch: seo/2026-09-portfolio-repair
to ship: open Claude Code and say "ship SEO item 3"
