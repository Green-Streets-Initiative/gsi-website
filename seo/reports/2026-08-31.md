## Findings

### 1. Google is indexing the UTM-tagged copy of our best town pages, not the canonical one

Our own internal links are the cause. The footer's Shift Towns column and the
nav dropdown render on every page, and every one of those links carried a
`?utm_content=` tag. That makes the tagged URL the most-linked version of the
page by a wide margin, and Google has followed the link graph rather than the
canonical tag.

Twelve weeks of page data:

| URL | Impr | Clicks | Clean twin in report |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/shift/towns/cambridge-ma?utm_content=footer_towns` | 337 | 2 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/everett-ma?utm_content=footer_towns` | 127 | 3 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/somerville-ma?utm_content=events_page_xlink` | 109 | 0 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/newton-ma?utm_content=footer_towns` | 80 | 0 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/watertown-ma?utm_content=footer_towns` | 70 | 0 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/boston-ma?utm_content=footer_towns` | 56 | 0 | **absent** |
| `/shift/towns/arlington-ma?utm_content=events_page_xlink` | 30 | 0 | 12 (split) |

809 impressions on parameterized URLs, and for six of our nine biggest town
pages the clean URL does not appear in the report at all. Arlington is the
instructive one: both variants rank, splitting one page's signals in two.

The canonical tags are correct and self-referencing — verified live on
`/shift/towns/cambridge-ma` both with and without the parameter. They are
simply losing to the internal link graph. Town pages are our strongest organic
asset, so this is worth fixing precisely because nothing looks broken.

Item 4 removes the tags from same-site links only. External campaign links are
untouched.

### 3. A used cargo e-bike price query is finding us — the used-car wedge, unclaimed

Two variants of `"tern gsd" used "$3000"` took 10 impressions at position 10
this week, and `/guides/what-you-can-do-with-an-e-cargo-bike` jumped from 10 to
38 impressions. Someone is pricing a used cargo e-bike and landing on us.

That is precisely the `used-car-to-ebike` audience arriving through a door we
did not build. No cluster pattern catches it (it buckets as noise), and no page
answers "what does a used cargo e-bike cost around here." One week of data is
too thin to act on, but it is the first organic sign of life from a wedge
audience that has shown zero impressions since the routine began. Flagging it
to watch, and to feed the September deep-dive's portfolio re-evaluation.

### Item 4 (new) — Stop pointing our own links at UTM-tagged town URLs

**What:** remove `?utm_content=` from same-site internal links —
`Footer.tsx` (10 town links), `Nav.tsx` (4 Shift-app dropdown links, 2 Towns
links), `TownsCrossLink.tsx` (chip strip, "All towns", teaser band). The
now-unused `utmContent` prop drops with its one caller in `events/page.tsx`.
External campaign links and `src/lib/utm.ts` are untouched.

**Why:** finding 1. Six of our biggest town pages have no clean URL in Search
Console at all; 809 impressions over 12 weeks are attributed to parameterized
copies, and Arlington's signals are split across two variants. Town pages are
the proven organic engine, so consolidating them onto their canonical URLs is
the highest-leverage cleanup available right now.

**What it costs:** a little PostHog attribution granularity — we lose which
module on a page a click came from. The referring pathname still tells us which
page. Nothing on the receiving pages reads `utm_content`; it is analytics-only.

**Verified:** `npx tsc --noEmit` clean; no `utm_content` remains in any
same-site link.
**Effort:** small · **Impact:** medium-high.
**Delivery:** branch `seo/2026-08-internal-utm-links`, pushed, not merged.
<https://github.com/Green-Streets-Initiative/gsi-website/compare/main...seo/2026-08-internal-utm-links>
**To ship:** merge the branch. Reindexing onto the clean URLs takes a few
weeks; watch the town-page rows in the page table.
